1Overview

SDR++ TETRA-SCHWARTZ is an experimental amateur radio edition of SDR++ for analysing TETRA networks. It receives with an SDR, decodes TETRA transmissions and measures the signal quality the way you know it from a test bench – from the constellation diagram to the coverage map.

Explicitly for experimental amateur radio purposesThe program is intended exclusively for experimental amateur radio purposes – for experiments, self-training and technical investigations in the amateur radio service. It is not a calibrated measuring instrument and is not intended for commercial, professional or public-authority use.

What the program does

  • Demodulation and decoding of TETRA (π/4-DQPSK, 18 ksym/s) with an 8-point constellation and ETSI tolerances.
  • Modulation quality: MER, EVM, I/Q offset, I/Q imbalance, phase/amplitude error, carrier and symbol clock offset, BER, CRC statistics.
  • Network information: cell, neighbour cells, network time, timeslot usage.
  • Stations and traffic in amateur radio mode with call signs (radioid.net) and talk groups.
  • TX analysis: spectrum, adjacent channel power, occupied bandwidth, shoulders, carrier leakage.
  • Burst analysis of handhelds: ramps, length, ripple, time mask, timing, ramp spectrum, noise on the uplink.
  • Multipath, frequency stability (Allan deviation) and reception limit.
  • Long-term monitoring: trends up to 24 h, CSV logs, alarms, measurement report.
  • Coverage measurement with a GPS mouse and maps for Google Earth and OpenStreetMap.
  • Several carriers compared at the same time.

How the program works

The SDR delivers a wide I/Q data stream. From it the program cuts several narrow “taps” (VFOs): the TETRA VFO for demodulator and decoder and – only when switched on – further, invisible taps for TX analysis, burst analysis, multipath and additional carriers. All taps must lie within the bandwidth of the SDR.

Legal notes

Only for experimental amateur radio and your own networks The program is explicitly intended for experimental amateur radio purposes: for amateur radio TETRA repeaters, your own test set-ups and measurements on your own equipment. In many countries – in Germany, for example, under § 5 TDDDG (formerly TTDSG) – it is prohibited to listen to transmissions of other radio services that are not intended for the general public or for you, and to pass on their content. This also applies to subscriber numbers, identities and text messages. Observe the regulations of your country. The program cannot transmit. These notes are not legal advice.
Experimental beta version Measured values are implemented with care but have not been compared with calibrated measuring instruments. Limits in the program are guide values – for binding statements always refer to the standard (ETSI EN 300 392-2, EN 300 394-1).

2Installation and first start

Windows

  1. Download the ZIP file SDRPP-TETRA-SCHWARTZ_v…_windows_x64 and extract it completely into a folder of its own (do not start it from inside the ZIP).
  2. Start sdrpp.exe. Because the program is not signed, Windows SmartScreen warns: More info → Run anyway. If the browser already blocks the download, explicitly keep the file in the download list.
  3. Settings, logs and name lists are kept in the same folder. For an update, extract the new folder next to the old one; the configuration (config.json, *_config.json) can be copied over.

./SDRPP-TETRA-SCHWARTZ-v0.19.0-beta-x86_64.AppImage

3The user interface

Areas

  • Top bar: start/stop, volume, frequency display (mouse wheel or click on the digits), VFO selection, signal level.
  • Menu column on the left: Source (SDR), TETRA Demodulator, Sinks (audio), Display, Theme, Module Manager. Every section can be expanded and collapsed and rearranged by dragging.
  • Spectrum and waterfall on the right. The TETRA VFO is the highlighted band; dragging with the mouse moves it, a click into the waterfall sets it to that frequency.

Hints

  • Almost every measured value has a tooltip: hold the mouse over it for a moment.
  • The width of the menu column can be changed; contents wrap instead of disappearing on the right.
  • Tables: move columns and change their width with the mouse; a right-click on the header shows or hides columns. The arrangement is saved.
  • Reordering sections in the TETRA demodulator: drag the grip (six dots on the right of the header); a line shows where the section lands. A right-click on the header offers Move up / Move down / Move to top / Move to bottom / Reset order. The order is saved.
  • Own window: the window icon left of the grip shows the section in its own window (icon blue = window open); a second click puts it back into the menu. The constellation is a section of its own. At the very top only audio and ETSI/Amateur radio remain.
  • Channel raster: TX analysis → 25 kHz channel raster draws dashed channel boundaries at ±12.5 and ±37.5 kHz around the TETRA channel in the spectrum. Smoothing of the spectrum: Display → FFT Smoothing.

Module Manager

In the Module Manager every module has two switches: Menu shows it in the menu column, On enables it. Below, the built-in menus (VFO Color, Debug, Band Plan, Display, Theme) can be shown, and further SDR types can be added to the source list under Sources in the source list. The SDR currently in use always stays visible.

Own windows

Layout presets (from v0.22.0): the button Layout... next to ETSI/Amateur radio arranges sections as tiles to the right of the waterfall: Measuring station (constellation, measurements, stations or trends), Drive test (GPS/map and measurements on the right, trends below) or Classic (everything in the menu). The tiles can be moved and resized; choosing the preset again puts them back.

Touch mode: Display → Touch mode (larger controls) enlarges buttons, sliders and scroll bars and gives touches more margin; dragging with the finger on an empty spot scrolls the menu and windows. Tapping a value shows its help. Below it, a scale suggestion for the screen is shown (size, resolution, pixel density; designed for 10–17 inch touch laptops); Use applies it after a restart. Same on Windows and Linux.

With the window icon in the header of a section, the constellation, stations, network, TX analysis, trends, carriers, bursts and multipath can be moved into separate, freely movable windows – handy on a second screen. The menu then shows Bring to front. Position and size are remembered.

4Setting up the SDR

Basic rules

  • Sample rate: 1–2.4 MS/s are enough for one carrier. For burst timing (uplink and downlink at the same time) and additional carriers the bandwidth must cover all frequencies – for 70 cm repeaters with 9.4 MHz duplex spacing that means ≥ 10 MS/s; otherwise make the measurements one after the other.
  • Do not put the centre on the carrier: the DC component of the SDR lies in the middle of the band. Tune the TETRA carrier at least ~100 kHz away from it, otherwise it falsifies the I/Q offset and carrier leakage.
  • Level: as much gain as needed, as little as possible. Auto RF gain looks for the best signal-to-noise ratio: it steps up by 3 dB while the ratio still improves and steps back once the noise floor only rises with the gain. It goes down at once when any signal in the whole SDR band (not only TETRA) reaches the ADC limit. The Band line shows this peak and the ratio.
  • Gain mode (from v0.20.4): Level and hold finds the best step once and then keeps it fixed – all results of a measurement series are taken with the same receiver settings. If the ADC nears clipping or the ratio drops by more than 6 dB, a note appears; Re-level levels again. Follow adjusts continuously. Automatic (GPS) (default) follows while moving (GPS above 5 km/h, averaged over 10 s) and holds after 30 s standing still or without GPS. With a level calibration (dBm) it may follow as well: the program remembers the gain when the calibration is entered and corrects the level by the difference from the SDR's gain table (about ±1 dB, shown as "gain-corrected"). Older calibrations without a remembered gain ask to be entered again; until then it holds.
  • Lock VFO: the mouse no longer tunes the TETRA VFO – clicking or dragging in the spectrum and waterfall only moves the view, the wheel and the arrow keys do nothing. AFC and frequency entry still work.
  • SDR line: shows source and sample rate and recommends a range (tooltip). Recommended view sets the centre to 440 MHz − 0.45 × sample rate so the band ends just above 440 MHz; this also happens automatically at the very first start.
  • Gain control locked: while Auto RF gain is active, the gain control in the source menu is locked ("Locked by TETRA Auto RF gain"). Release switches the automatic gain off and frees the control.
  • Warm-up: wait 10–15 minutes after switching on before calibrating the frequency or measuring stability.

Calibration

In Measurements, Carrier vs. SYSINFO shows how far the received carrier deviates from the frequency the base station itself announces. Calibrate SDR sets the frequency correction of the SDR so that this deviation disappears.

Calibrate only on a trusted cellThe value contains the error of the base station and of the SDR. Only calibrate on a station whose frequency you know (GPS-disciplined, or measured beforehand with a reference-locked SDR), and only after the warm-up.

Level in dBm

The SDR measures in dBFS (relative to full scale). Level cal. (dB) converts this to dBm: feed in a signal of known power (signal generator) and set the value so that the display is correct. The calibration only applies to the same gain and frequency – switch Auto RF gain off for it.

5TETRA demodulator: basics

  1. Choose the SDR in Source and start it with ▶.
  2. Find the TETRA carrier in the waterfall (a 25 kHz wide, uniform band) and drag the TETRA VFO onto it or enter the frequency. The AFC pulls the VFO along as soon as the decoder locks.
  3. Choose the operating mode: Amateur radio (duplex tables for 70 cm, call signs, talk groups) or ETSI.
  4. Check: RF status “OK”, MER above ~15 dB, MCC/MNC and frequencies appear in the network section.

Constellation

π/4-DQPSK has eight target points (8 points (pi/4)) – switched off, they are folded onto four. The rings show the ETSI limits (EVM 10 % rms, 30 % peak). The points turn green when the measured EVM is within the limit.

Remove I/Q offset removes a carrier leakage of the transmitter (or the DC component of the receiver) from the display. The measurements still show it.

Decoder mode

The program decodes by itself (normal case). At the very bottom of the menu, the closed section NETSYMS symbol streaming (advanced) sends the symbols over UDP to an external program. While it is active, the built-in decoder and all measurements that need it are off; a note at the top of the menu says so. From v0.20.1 on, the program starts with the built-in decoder if NETSYMS was selected but not sending.

The TETRA demodulator header no longer has a checkbox to switch it off (a stray click switched the whole decoder off). If the module is switched off anyway, the checkbox appears again.

8Stations and traffic

In amateur radio mode, Stations & Traffic collects all subscribers and groups:

  • Stations: ISSI (in amateur radio = DMR ID; 7 digits user, 6 digits repeater), call sign and location from radioid.net, timeslot, last heard, scanned groups, activity.
  • Groups: GSSI with the name of the talk group (Brandmeister), current and last speaker, registered stations.
  • Traffic: call set-up, transmission grant, SDS text messages and positions in plain text.

Download name lists loads the name lists (radioid.net and Brandmeister) into the configuration folder; without internet the files can also be placed there by hand. Hide call control hides control messages so that only SDS and status remain.

9TX analysis (spectrum)

Run analysis (250 kHz tap) starts a 250 kHz wide tap around the channel and measures:

  • Channel power through the TETRA filter.
  • ACP at ±25, ±50, ±75 kHz in dBc, with (adjustable) limits.
  • Occupied BW (99 %) and −26 dB bandwidth compared with the ideal TETRA spectrum.
  • Shoulders and edge slope – shoulders point to non-linearity of the power amplifier.
  • Carrier line – carrier leakage as a spectral line.

With a large I/Q offset, a diagnosis line names the likely cause: if carrier line and I/Q offset agree and the receiver's DC is far enough away, it is carrier leakage of the transmitter (check its LO/DC calibration).

RBW and Avg set resolution and averaging. Store floor stores the noise of the receiver (transmitter off or input terminated with 50 Ω); values close to this limit are marked grey.

Limits of the measurementThe adjacent channel measurement is limited by the dynamic range and phase noise of the SDR – typically −60 to −70 dBc. For values close to the limit, use a strong, clean signal and a low gain.

10Trends, timeslots, stability, reception limit

Over time

The Trends & timeslots section keeps the last 24 hours with one value per second: level, MER, CRC errors, carrier offset, traffic per timeslot and bursts/s. For long time spans every pixel combines several seconds; line = mean, band = minimum to maximum, red marks = sync losses. The mouse shows the values at any point in time.

Open a CSV log opens a saved log in the same view – so measurements over several days can be evaluated, too. Back to live returns to the running measurement.

Timeslot usage

Every downlink burst announces what its timeslot is used for. The table counts traffic, assigned control, common control and free per timeslot, plus the uplink traffic. Frame 18 (always control) is left out.

Frequency stability

The Frequency stability tab shows the deviation between the received carrier and the SYSINFO frequency in ppb over time, and the Allan deviation: how much the frequency fluctuates on average over 1 s, 2 s, 4 s … A decrease with the averaging time is normal measurement noise; a rise at long times shows drift (temperature, ageing).

Meaningful only with a referenceThe value contains base station and SDR. Without a GPS-disciplined reference at the SDR you mainly measure your own crystal.

Reading the chart: the frequency over time at the top, the Allan deviation on a log-log scale below. The longest section without gaps is evaluated; seconds without synchronisation interrupt it.

Level vs. quality – reception limit

The Level vs. quality tab sorts all seconds by level (1 dB steps) and shows CRC errors and MER for each step. The reception limit is the lowest level above which no step exceeds the CRC limit (default 1 %). It is a practical threshold for coverage maps. With a calibrated level (dBm) it is comparable between measurements.

11Additional carriers

Carriers (multi-carrier) evaluates up to eight additional carriers within the SDR bandwidth at the same time: level, distance to the main carrier, MER, carrier offset and synchronisation – only the main carrier is decoded.

  • Enter a frequency → Add carrier, or Add neighbour cells takes the neighbour cells from the network information.
  • The level history of all carriers appears below (10 min to 24 h).
  • While a CSV log is running, …_carriers.csv is written as well.

Each carrier costs about as much CPU time as another VFO.

12Burst analysis (uplink)

Handhelds transmit in bursts – short transmissions in their timeslot. The burst analysis measures them through a 25 kHz channel filter. The frequency is the uplink from the network information (Uplink (SYSINFO)), a manual frequency or the TETRA VFO (e.g. a base station in discontinuous mode).

Burst shape and time mask

  • Length (−3 dB): burst length; nominal 231 symbols (12.8 ms) for normal, 103 for half bursts.
  • Ramp up/down: time from −20 to −1 dBc and back.
  • Ripple: ripple in the useful part (averaged).
  • Outside the burst: power before and after the burst (dBc) – limited by the receiver noise (“at the floor”).

The chart shows the shape averaged over the last bursts with the Time mask (red). The mask values are adjustable and only guide values.

Timing relative to the downlink

With Timing vs. downlink the program opens a second, sample-aligned tap on the TETRA VFO (downlink of the same cell), finds the slot grid there and measures when each burst rises after the start of its uplink timeslot. Most important is the comparison between radios and the spread; the absolute value also contains propagation differences (3.3 µs per km of detour).

Ramp spectrum (switching transients)

Most of the interference in the adjacent channels is produced when switching on and off. Ramp spectrum measures the channel power at −50, −25, ±0, +25, +50 kHz in 1.8 ms windows during the ramp-up, in the middle and during the ramp-down, relative to the middle. Orange: ramp more than 6 dB above the middle (splatter).

As in the standard (from v0.21.1): the line Switching transients (peak, ±25 kHz) measures, as in EN 300 394-1 7.1.4, the peak power through the TETRA filter at −25 and +25 kHz over the whole ramp-up and ramp-down period, with maximum hold since the last reset. Limit −50 dBc, for MS power classes 4/4L −45 dBc (selection under Time mask). The envelope of the burst analysis now runs through the TETRA filter (root raised cosine 0.35, 33 symbols) as EN 300 392-2 6.4.5 requires. Time mask checks table 6.8 / figure 6.3: ramp-up within t1 (16 symbols), ramp-down within t3 (15 symbols), at most +6 dBc during t1 and +3 dBc during t3, outside Lmin (MS −70 dBc or −36 dBm, BS −40 dBc). If the measurement floor is above Lmin, this is shown. Default averaging: 200 bursts (EN 300 394-1 8.4). For the adjacent channel power, ACP limits offers presets per tables 7.1–7.3.

Noise on the uplink

Noise between bursts measures the power in the channel while nobody transmits – on the uplink of a repeater that is its receive noise and interference. The history over up to 24 h shows interferers that make the repeater “deaf”; an alarm can react to it.

Never connect a transmitter directlyNever connect a handheld to the SDR without sufficient attenuation (e.g. 60–70 dB, power-rated attenuator). For measurements an antenna a few metres away is usually enough.

13Multipath

Every downlink burst contains a known training sequence. From it, Multipath (downlink) estimates the channel impulse response and averages it into a power delay profile. The comparison with the profile of an ideal signal (green) reveals echoes:

  • Strongest echo: delay (µs), detour (km) and level of the strongest echo above −15 dB.
  • Extra delay spread: additional delay spread compared with the ideal signal.

Echoes from about 55 µs (17 km detour) to 222 µs are detected; shorter ones only widen the main path. TETRA receivers cope with echoes up to about ¼ symbol (14 µs) without loss. Averaging: 2 s for drives, 60 s or unlimited for fixed locations.

15Measurement report

Report → Create report creates an HTML file in the log folder and opens it in the browser. It contains:

  • summary (level, MER, EVM, CRC, sync losses, frequency offset),
  • cell and key values, histories as charts,
  • timeslot usage, reception limit, Allan deviation,
  • TX analysis, burst analysis with profile, multipath, additional carriers,
  • the latest events.

The period is the time span selected under Trends (or the loaded log). Location, antenna or occasion can be entered under Notes. As PDF: print in the browser → “Save as PDF”.

16GPS, coverage measurement and maps

With a GPS mouse every second of the CSV log gets a position. From this, coverage maps with coloured measurement points and a heatmap are created.

Connecting the GPS mouse

  1. Connect the GPS mouse (USB or serial, NMEA output). On Windows it appears as a COM port, on Linux usually as /dev/ttyACM0 or /dev/ttyUSB0 (add the user to the dialout group).
  2. Under GPS & coverage map choose the port (Rescan refreshes the list), set the baud rate (usually 4800 or 9600) and press Connect.
  3. Wait until position, number of satellites and HDOP are shown. With “Waiting for NMEA data”, port or baud rate are wrong.

Measurement drive

  1. Let the decoder lock onto the desired cell and press Lock current cell: seconds in which another cell or no cell is received then count as “no service”.
  2. Calibrate the level if possible (Level cal.) and switch Auto RF gain off for comparable values, or evaluate the level in dBm.
  3. Start CSV log and drive off. Antenna firmly on the roof, same set-up as on earlier drives.
  4. After the drive, Stop CSV log.

Creating maps

Map value selects the colour scale: Level (−115…−65 dBm or −95…−45 dBFS), MER (5…30 dB), BER or Quality (CRC) – green up to the CRC limit from chapter 10, yellow up to ten times that, red above; black = no service. Export map (KML + OSM) writes two files next to the log:

In the browser (OpenStreetMap)

Open …_level_map.html with a double-click (internet connection needed for the map tiles). At the top right the layers Points, Heatmap and No service only can be switched; a click on a point shows time, level, MER, BER and CRC errors.

In Google Earth

Open …_level.kml in Google Earth (desktop, or web: Projects → Import KML file). Every point is coloured, the description contains the measured values. In Google Earth Pro the file can be combined with elevation profile and terrain.

Own evaluations

The CSV log (columns lat, lon, level_dbm …) can be opened directly in QGIS (Delimited text, separator semicolon) or a spreadsheet, e.g. for your own heatmaps or profiles along a route.

Good heatmapsDrive slowly or cover the same route several times – every second is one point. At high speed the points are far apart. Do not mix measurements with different antennas or gain.

17Measurement scenarios

The following procedures are suggestions – they can be combined freely.

A · Transmitter quality of a repeater (downlink)

Goal: modulation quality and spectrum of the base station · duration: 10 minutes
  1. SDR in line of sight or with a directional antenna; signal well above the noise (stable MER display). Band centre ≥ 100 kHz away from the carrier.
  2. Let Auto RF gain settle, then read MER, EVM, I/Q offset, IRR and error split.
  3. Run analysis of the TX analysis; beforehand Store floor with the transmitter off (or the input terminated).
  4. Assess ACP, occupied bandwidth, shoulders and carrier leakage; for values close to the measurement limit lower the gain.
  5. Create report for documentation.

Interpretation: high I/Q offset with poor MER but good MER w/o offset → carrier leakage of the modulator. Much phase error → phase noise of the oscillator. Pronounced shoulders → overdriven power amplifier.

B · Frequency accuracy of the base station

Goal: frequency error in ppb and stability · requires a GPS-disciplined SDR (e.g. Pluto+ with 10 MHz)
  1. Connect the SDR to the reference, warm up for at least 15 minutes.
  2. Watch Carrier vs. SYSINFO (target: ±200 ppb according to ETSI).
  3. Under Trends → Frequency stability let it run for at least one hour; read the Allan deviation.
  4. If required, let the measurement run overnight (CSV log) and open the log later.

C · Coverage measurement with heatmap

Goal: coverage map and practical reception limit
  1. Calibrate the level (dBm), Auto RF gain off, connect GPS, lock the cell (chapter 16).
  2. Start the CSV log, drive through the area – also to the edge of the coverage.
  3. Open the log under Trends → Level vs. quality: read the reception limit and set the CRC limit accordingly.
  4. Export and compare the Level and Quality (CRC) maps; measure gaps (black) again specifically.

D · Long-term monitoring of a repeater

Goal: detect outages and degradations · duration: days
  1. Set up a fixed receiver with stable gain (Auto RF gain off).
  2. Alarms: switch on level, MER, CRC and sync, hold time e.g. 30 s, “Start CSV log on alarm”.
  3. Optionally the burst analysis on the uplink for the noise level and its alarm.
  4. Look at the trends daily or open the log; in case of anomalies Create report.

E · Checking a handheld (uplink)

Goal: burst shape, timing and splatter of a radio
  1. Set the SDR so that downlink and uplink are within the bandwidth; TETRA VFO on the downlink, decoder locked.
  2. Start Burst analysis with source Uplink (SYSINFO), switch on Timing vs. downlink and Ramp spectrum.
  3. Make a few transmissions with the radio (or send an SDS); receiver close to the radio (mind the attenuation!).
  4. Check burst length, ramps, ripple and mask; compare timing and spread with other radios; look at the ramp spectrum for splatter.

F · Hunting interference at the repeater receiver

Goal: detect intermittent interferers on the uplink
  1. Run the burst analysis on the uplink; note Noise between bursts as baseline.
  2. Set the alarm “UL noise above” a few dB above the baseline.
  3. Look for time patterns in the history (Noise history, 24 h) – e.g. daily times point to devices in the neighbourhood.

G · Comparing neighbour cells

  1. Decoder on one cell; neighbour cells appear under Network & cell.
  2. Carriers → Add neighbour cells.
  3. Compare level differences and MER over time (e.g. from the same location at different times of day).

H · Multipath at a new location

  1. Decoder locked, MER above 15 dB.
  2. Start Multipath with Averaging 60 s; note echoes and delay spread.
  3. On drives choose 2 s and compare places with strong echoes with poor MER.

Timing: “slot grid not found”The TETRA VFO must be on the downlink of the same cell and locked.

Overload check: RECEIVER OVERLOADEDThe downlink gets weaker during the bursts: the strong radio overloads the receiver (analog, before the ADC; lowering the gain in the software is often not enough). Increase the distance, add an attenuator (30–40 dB) or use a coupler and dummy load. Limit: 1 dB, averaged over 20 bursts; needs the downlink in the SDR span.
Pluto shows up as a drive but not in SDR++udev rule missing (chapter 2.3); then Refresh.

Checking USB and lost samples

Poor MER with a clean spectrum and a rising Re-locks with timing slip count means samples are missing. Narrow it down like this (PlutoSDR on Linux; quit SDR++ first):

  1. Device and USB alone: set the rate and read a fixed number of samples while timing it. 40 million samples at 4 MS/s must take 10.0 s: 
    iio_attr -u usb: -c ad9361-phy voltage0 sampling_frequency 4000000
time iio_readdev -u usb: -b 262144 -s 40000000 cf-ad9361-lpc voltage0 voltage1 > /dev/null
    Clearly more than 10 s means the Pluto drops data (USB, cable, power). Test the limit with 7 MS/s and -s 70000000.
  2. Connection: keep sudo dmesg -w running. disconnect or error -71 point to the cable, a contact or too little power (connect the Pluto's second USB port to a power supply). lsusb -t shows which devices share the bus (webcam, card reader).
  3. In SDR++: press Reset totals and let it run for two minutes. Overflows in the source menu must stay at 0. If it rises only here, the computer cannot keep up: close screen recording and other programs, lower the sample rate.

Glossary

AACHAccess Assignment Channel – in every downlink burst; announces the use of the timeslots.
ACPAdjacent Channel Power – power in the adjacent channel, in dBc.
AFCAutomatic frequency control of the VFO.
Allan deviationMeasure of frequency fluctuations as a function of the averaging time τ.
dBc / dBFS / dBmrelative to the carrier / to full scale / to 1 mW.
EVM, MERError Vector Magnitude, Modulation Error Ratio – measures of modulation errors.
GSSI, ISSIGroup and individual subscriber identity (in amateur radio the DMR ID).
MCC, MNC, CCCountry, network and colour code of the cell.
NTSNormal Training Sequence – known bit pattern in every burst (synchronisation, channel estimation).
OBWOccupied Bandwidth – bandwidth containing 99 % of the power.
SDSShort Data Service – text messages, status, positions (LIP).
